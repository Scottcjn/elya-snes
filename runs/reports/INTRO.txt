THE STUDY BEHIND THAT CONFIGURATION
All errors measured by decoding the emitted stream and comparing
against the ffmpeg source frames -- not against the encoder's own
intermediate state, which would only prove it agrees with itself.
================================================================

COLOUR -- 8 palettes against 1, which is what the Genesis can do

  arm                      mean    p50     p95     p99     max   bad tiles
  1 palette (Genesis)     799.9  155.4  4664.0  5815.3  9002.2  14.35%
  8pal one-shot           188.6   56.0   756.3  1698.3  3254.1   0.73%
  8pal Lloyd, luma seed   258.3   51.9  1203.0  1919.1  3697.9   0.69%
  8pal Lloyd, kmeans seed 161.8   49.4   652.9  1212.0  2590.4   0.16%   <- ships

  A 4bpp tilemap entry already carries three palette bits, so per-tile
  palette choice costs NOTHING in tile data -- only 256 B of CGRAM for the
  whole clip.  4.9x lower colour error and 90x fewer bad tiles.  What it
  does cost is ROM: 664 KiB against 482, because finer colour makes more
  tiles distinct and fewer of them collapse.

  The colour win is paid for in cartridge, not in bandwidth.

SEEDING -- the same algorithm, twice, once worse than not running it

  Lloyd's algorithm on palette assignment (assign tiles to their best
  palette, rebuild each palette from the tiles that chose it, repeat)
  IMPROVED on one seed and REGRESSED on another:

    seeded by equal-count luma buckets   258.3 mean -- WORSE than one-shot
    seeded by k-means on tile means      161.8 mean -- better than one-shot

  Equal-count buckets are wrong for a clip that is mostly one dark colour:
  they split the dark into groups that did not need splitting and starve
  everything else.  Convergence was fast and confident either way -- three
  rounds, monotone -- which is exactly what a bad local optimum looks like.

THREE THINGS THAT DID NOT WORK, MEASURED

  1. Stable slot allocation.  Predicted to "cut the delta substantially"
     against the Genesis encoder's per-frame rebuild.  Measured: 5,601 vs
     5,691 B/frame, a 1.6% win.  On this clip 83% of tiles change every
     frame anyway, and when almost everything changes, keeping a tile in
     its slot buys almost nothing.

  2. Lloyd with the obvious seed.  Above.  Worse than not refining.

  3. Neighbour palette bias, to close the visible seams.  Measured on the
     metric that can actually see them -- excess boundary jump, where the
     SOURCE is smooth across a tile edge and the RENDER is not:

       arm                excess jump mean   p99    >16
       1 palette                 1.40      28.33   3.20%
       8 pal, bias 0             1.76      26.67   2.72%
       8 pal, bias 0.25          1.75      26.67   2.68%

     0.04 percentage points.  Defaulted off.

  Note the shape of (3): per-tile error said the artifact was fixed --
  "bad tiles" fell 0.73% -> 0.16% -- while the picture still showed flat
  rectangles.  A per-tile budget scores every tile in isolation and the
  defect lives BETWEEN tiles, so it cannot see it at any threshold.

  And note what the right metric then said: 8 palettes are slightly WORSE
  than 1 on mean boundary jump (1.76 vs 1.40), because one palette has no
  palette seams at all, only banding.  8 palettes buy far fewer LARGE
  jumps and 4.9x the colour accuracy.  "Better colour, marginally more
  seaming" is the honest summary, not "better".

WHAT IS UNTESTED

  Dithering.  Ordered dither would break up the flat regions that still
  band, and it is the standard answer to exactly this.  It is also likely
  to cost real ROM, because a dither pattern makes neighbouring tiles
  distinct that would otherwise have deduplicated.  Not measured, so not
  claimed either way.

  A bigger window.  256x128 encodes and fits the bandwidth (49% of the
  tile budget) but needs 1024 double-buffered slots against a cap of
  exactly 1024, and 1,193 KiB of ROM.  Zero margin on the slot table is
  not a place to ship from.

  Everything above is a HOST measurement.  No 65816 has played this
  stream: the player is not written yet.  The vblank budget it is all
  sized against is computed from the master clock, not observed on the
  console.
